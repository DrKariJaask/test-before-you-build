# Market Validation: Process, Methods and Examples

**By [Dr. Kari Jääskeläinen](https://testbeforeyoubuild.com/about-kari-jaaskelainen/)**  
Published and updated 16 September 2026

Market validation examines whether a defined group of potential buyers or users has a meaningful problem, how they address it today and what would need to be true for them to adopt a new solution. The purpose is to replace critical assumptions with decision-relevant evidence before major development investment.

## Market validation in one sentence

Define the commercial decision, identify the assumptions that matter most, gather the right evidence from the right market and use explicit criteria to decide whether to BUILD, CHANGE or KILL.

## What market validation means

Market validation is the structured collection and analysis of evidence about a proposed market opportunity. It asks whether the intended customers experience the proposed problem, whether it matters enough to influence behaviour, how existing alternatives perform and whether the conditions for buying or adopting a new solution are credible.

Validation is not one survey, one interview or one favourable metric. It is a chain of evidence connected to the assumptions behind a real decision. A method is useful only when it tests an assumption that could change what you decide to do.

Market research describes the wider environment: customers, competitors, market size, trends and constraints. Market validation tests whether the conditions for a specific commercial idea are credible. Product validation examines whether a proposed solution works for customers, for example through concept, prototype or usability testing.

Market validation can reduce uncertainty, expose incorrect assumptions, reveal segment differences and show what evidence supports the next investment. It cannot guarantee future sales or remove the risks of execution, competition and market change.

## The market validation process

1. **Define the decision.** State the investment or direction the evidence must inform.
2. **Map the critical assumptions.** List what must be true about the customer, problem, alternatives, value, buying process, feasibility and commercial model.
3. **Define the target market precisely.** Identify realistic buyers, users, influencers and decision-makers by role, context, current behaviour and trigger.
4. **Review existing evidence.** Examine market data, competitors, substitutes, search behaviour, regulation and internal customer knowledge.
5. **Select methods for the assumptions.** Use discovery methods for unknown problems and experiments requiring commitment when the question concerns adoption or willingness to pay.
6. **Collect and analyse evidence.** Look for recurring signals, disagreements, segment differences, exceptions and barriers.
7. **Make and document the decision.** Explain what supports BUILD, what requires CHANGE, what supports KILL and what remains unresolved.

The sequence is iterative. New evidence may change the target group, reveal a different problem or create a new assumption that needs its own test.

## Market validation methods and when to use them

- **Desk and competitor research:** market structure, trends, alternatives, regulation and possible segments.
- **Search and behavioural data:** customer language, existing interest, journeys and observable behaviour.
- **Customer discovery interviews:** problems, consequences, current workarounds, decision criteria and barriers.
- **Surveys:** testing the prevalence or distribution of well-defined findings in a larger group.
- **Concept or prototype tests:** comprehension, perceived relevance, workflow and usability.
- **Landing-page or message tests:** which promise attracts attention and a measurable next action.
- **Pilots or concierge tests:** real use, delivery requirements, measurable value and organisational adoption.
- **Pre-sales, deposits or procurement steps:** willingness to commit money, budget, time, reputation or process.

When little is known about customers’ actual situations, direct interviews are usually more informative than a large closed-question survey. Interviews can uncover language, workflows, alternatives and decision factors that the research team did not know to include in a questionnaire.

As uncertainty moves from the problem to adoption, the evidence should move closer to behaviour. A prototype, pilot, pre-sale or procurement discussion can test conditions that interviews alone cannot settle.

## How to judge the strength of market-validation evidence

- **Relevance:** Did the evidence come from realistic buyers, users or decision-makers?
- **Specificity:** Does it concern actual problems and behaviour rather than broad opinions?
- **Commitment:** Did the customer risk time, budget, process, reputation or money?
- **Consistency:** Do signals recur, and can contradictions or segment differences be explained?
- **Decision fit:** Is the evidence strong enough for this particular next investment?

Negative and contradictory evidence is valuable. A weak problem, satisfied customers, inaccessible buyers, prohibitive integration requirements or an unworkable procurement route can prevent a costly mistake.

## Three illustrative market validation examples

These are hypothetical scenarios, not client case studies or claimed results.

### B2B software

A team believes operations managers need software to replace a spreadsheet-based process. Market research identifies alternatives. Interviews with users, budget holders and IT reveal the consequences of the problem and barriers to adoption. A limited pilot then tests whether one workflow creates measurable value.

Possible decision: CHANGE the broad platform into a narrower workflow product for one segment.

### Research-based innovation

A university team has developed a measurement technology. Interviews distinguish laboratory users, equipment manufacturers, procurement teams and organisations that carry the financial consequence of inaccurate measurement. The evidence tests whether the strongest route is a product, component, licence or partnership.

Possible decision: BUILD a partner-led demonstration instead of manufacturing a complete product.

### Consumer service

Interviews examine when the problem occurs, how consumers solve it today and what prevents change. A landing page compares value propositions, followed where appropriate by a paid or deposit-based test.

Possible decision: KILL the original positioning or CHANGE the target situation before developing the service platform.

## What the market validation output should contain

- The commercial decision and assumptions tested.
- The target definition and participant-selection logic.
- The methods used and why they fit the questions.
- Supporting and contradictory evidence and segment differences.
- Current alternatives, buying criteria and barriers to change.
- Limitations and unresolved questions.
- Recommended changes and next tests.
- A clear BUILD, CHANGE or KILL recommendation.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is market validation?

Market validation is the process of gathering and analysing evidence to determine whether a defined customer group has a meaningful problem, whether the conditions for adopting a proposed solution are credible and whether the evidence justifies the next commercial investment.

### What are the main market validation methods?

Common methods include desk and competitor research, search and behavioural data, customer interviews, surveys, concept or prototype tests, landing-page experiments, pilots, pre-sales and procurement discussions.

### Is market validation the same as market research?

No. Market research describes a market. Market validation tests the specific assumptions behind an idea and connects the evidence to a decision.

### Can interviews validate a market?

Relevant, well-conducted interviews can provide meaningful evidence about customer problems, current behaviour, buying criteria and barriers to change. Some decisions should also be tested with behavioural evidence.

### How long does market validation take?

The timeline depends on the question, target group and methods. Test Before You Build’s standard 20-interview project typically takes approximately 2–4 weeks. Broader studies are scoped separately.

### What should a market validation report include?

A useful report states the decision and assumptions tested, explains participant selection and methods, presents supporting and contradictory evidence, identifies limitations, recommends changes and concludes with a clear next decision.
